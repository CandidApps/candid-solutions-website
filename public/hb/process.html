Program Overview

Why this program is critical

States are actively legislating how dental practices may handle third-party financing. Illinois is the clearest example: as of January 1, 2025, a practice cannot complete any part of a patient's application, cannot submit it, and cannot even hand the patient a tablet or device to apply on in the office. California has enacted similar restrictions, and industry counsel expect more states to follow.

That makes patient-initiated application the only compliant path — and it's exactly what Patient Notify delivers. Signage the patient scans or texts on their own phone, a double opt-in they control, and an application that never touches office hardware or staff hands.

Adoption isn't a nice-to-have. In regulated states it's the difference between offering financing legally and not offering it at all — and as this becomes the national standard, every Happen Bank location will need it.

What Illinois actually prohibits — effective Jan 1, 2025

No office devices

Practices may not provide a patient an electronic device to apply for third-party financing in the office.

No staff assistance

Staff may not complete any portion of an application, nor collect or submit it on the patient's behalf.

No brokering

Practices may not arrange, broker, or establish third-party financing for a patient.

Required disclosure

A statutory written disclosure must be given whenever financing is discussed. Penalties start at $500 and rise to $1,000 per additional violation.

Where this already applies — and where it's heading

Illinois

The strictest to date. No office devices, no staff assistance, no brokering, and practice-branded application links are restricted. Effective Jan 1, 2025.

California

Has enacted similar restrictions on how practices may facilitate third-party patient financing.

Connecticut

Broadly covers all health care providers — dental, medical, vision. Practices may not complete or submit a patient's application; violations are an unfair trade practice. Effective Jan 1, 2027.

New York

Patient-initiated, own-device application is the expectation for third-party financing in practice settings.

Next wave

Additional states are enacting or actively considering similar legislation. Industry counsel expect meaningful nationwide impact if it continues to spread.

Where orders come from

Two sources feed the same monthly run: new offices uploaded by Happen Bank (or requested at partner@candid.solutions), and reorders that existing offices submit themselves through the customer portal.

The monthly rhythm

Orders are batched on the 1st Monday of each month (with an optional 3rd-Monday run), invoiced within two business days, and shipped within five business days of payment — so every location follows the same predictable cadence.

Client portal, direct messaging & the compliant SMS/QR flow

Portal invitation & login

Each office contact is provisioned a Client Portal account, timed with their shipment. User onboarding/offboarding is managed by us.

Direct messaging tool

Staff can send an application link directly to a named patient by email, tracked Sent → Opened → Link Clicked, with one follow-up allowed.

SMS volume & QR generation

500k SMS/year included; +250k blocks are $3,000. QR codes are generated in bulk and automatically per new office.

Patient-initiated SMS/QR is a one-time conversation — no marketing follow-up is currently sent after opt-in.

Honest Assessment

The program hasn't hit its potential yet

Awareness in-office

Materials aren't always displayed consistently; front-desk turnover means new staff often aren't aware of the program.

Patient awareness

Patients don't always notice signage or understand what scanning the QR code will do for them.

Reinforcement

Little proactive follow-up with offices that go quiet — low-engagement locations aren't flagged.

!

Email deliverability — sender domain mismatch

The "From" address currently comes from the Patient Notify domain — not LendingClub or Happen Bank — yet the messages reference LendingClub. That mismatch trips spam filters at both the office and patient level: mail servers read it as potential spoofing, so messages are frequently flagged and land in spam rather than the inbox.

!

Separate login from the LendingClub portal

Patient Notify is a separate application from the LendingClub portal, so offices end up with two logins — and many don't realize the second one exists. Ideally the tool would be embedded directly into their existing LendingClub portal so there's a single place to work.

!

Limited reporting, automation & customization for offices

Offices have little reporting capability, automation, or customization in the portal today, so there's not much pulling them back in to use it. Richer self-serve reporting and configurable automation would give staff a reason to log in and stay engaged.

!

Reporting is disconnected from the current location portal

Because Patient Notify's reporting isn't connected to the office's current location portal, the program's true success and conversion rate is hard to identify. These two platforms should communicate — and once they do, the system will know when a patient hasn't completed their application, enabling automated follow-ups.

!

No magic-link login

Requiring an email and password is friction that keeps busy front-desk staff from logging in. Adding magic-link logins — where a location signs in from a single emailed link, no password to remember — would remove that barrier and drive more consistent portal usage.

!

Low license utilization

Current license usage is low — many provisioned seats sit idle. We should focus on maximizing the licenses already in place, and automatically disable unused licenses so the program's active footprint stays accurate and every seat is working.
