Our Observations
ℹ️300 Mbps symmetrical fiber is a solid commercial-grade connection. For a church with ~10 staff and occasional streaming/admin work, this is comfortably above typical needs — 100/100 Mbps would also be sufficient.
⚠️5 static IPs ($20/mo) is significantly more than a church requires. Static IPs are useful for hosting public services (websites, mail servers, VPN endpoints). Most churches need 0-1. This appears to be a sales-driven add-on with no operational justification.
🔍Market context: Competitively priced 300/300 symmetrical fiber in the Aurora area typically ranges $80-$150/mo for business customers. MetroNet's $129.95 is in line with market, though wholesale partners and direct-fiber resellers may offer 15-25% better rates at renewal.

Our Observations
🚨The HPBX upgrade in March 2024 nearly doubled the church's monthly cost — from the original $351/mo (April 2023) to $603.55/mo. That's a 72% increase, with a full 36-month term restart locking the church in through March 2027.
⚠️Premium Attendant ($15/mo) and Expansion Module ($14/mo) appear over-specified. The Hosted Access Fee already includes a basic auto-attendant. The Expansion Module is a side-car panel useful for a high-volume receptionist managing many simultaneous lines — uncommon need for a church.
📈Industry benchmark (2025-2026 data): Hosted PBX market rates run $20-$45 per seat/month for basic-to-premium plans. MetroNet's $24.95/Standard Seat is in line with market, but the stacked add-ons (Hosted Access, Premium Attendant, Expansion, e550 upgrade) push the effective per-user cost above $40/seat all-in. Competitors like RingCentral, Vonage, and Dialpad bundle these features at $25-35/seat with no add-ons.
🔍The $127.80/mo gap between contract and invoice is meaningful. The signed contract maxes out at $504.90/mo for phone; the actual invoice is $377.10. This suggests roughly 5 Standard Seats (5 x $24.95 = $124.75) were dropped at some point. We recommend requesting an itemized current-state inventory from MetroNet to confirm exactly what's active vs. what's still being contracted.

Legitimate Early-Termination Paths (per MetroNet Business T&Cs)
⚖️ Material Adverse Price Change (the most realistic path)

MetroNet's T&Cs grant them the right to raise rates for regulatory or vendor cost changes. However: if such a change "considerably increases the price of the Fiber Services," the customer may terminate the affected service with no further liability by delivering written notice within 30 days of the notification.

Source: MetroNet Business Terms & Conditions, "Adjustments" section
🚨 SLA Failures / Material Service Breach

Chronic outages or repeated SLA failures can constitute material breach. Document every outage (date, time, duration, business impact). MetroNet doesn't publicly post a specific business SLA percentage, but standard fiber business SLAs run 99.9% uptime with credits for missed targets. If service is genuinely unreliable, this becomes leverage.

Action item: Maintain a service log starting today.
🔐 MetroNet Ceases Providing Service

Per T&Cs: "if for any reason such that it can no longer provide Fiber Services to you, this Agreement and Metronet's obligation to provide Fiber Services automatically terminates without further obligation." Rare but worth knowing.

📍 Move Outside the MetroNet Footprint

If the church relocates to an address MetroNet does not serve, the contract becomes voidable. Not applicable here, but worth noting if the church ever sells or relocates the building.

⚡ Force Majeure / Extended Outages

Standard force majeure language applies. Extended power, broadband, or network failures (even non-MetroNet) can suspend obligations. Note: the E-911 disclosure explicitly states 911 service is unavailable during power outages or internet disruptions — a meaningful risk for a building with elevator and life-safety systems.

Early Termination Liability (if walking away without cause)
💰Base ETL formula (per T&Cs): Remaining months × Monthly Recurring Charge (MRC) + any non-recurring fees + any costs MetroNet incurred from suppliers in connection with the service.

As of June 2026, that's roughly 11 months × $656 = ~$7,218 if terminating today through the May 2027 latest expiration. MetroNet may add equipment/installation cost recovery on top of this. The math gets cheaper every month — but waiting for natural expiration costs nothing.
